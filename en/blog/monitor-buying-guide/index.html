How to choose a monitor for work or gaming

A good monitor depends on what you connect to it, how far away you sit and which tasks occupy most of your day.

← All English guides

1. Choose size and resolution together

Resolution controls how much detail a panel can show, while screen size influences pixel density and viewing distance. A 24-inch Full HD monitor can suit a compact desk; 27-inch QHD models often offer a useful balance of workspace and sharpness. A 4K panel can be attractive for detailed content, but demands more graphics processing power in games.

Do not judge a monitor only by inches. Think about text sharpness, scaling, desk depth and whether you need to display several windows.

2. Match refresh rate to the actual connection

A 144 Hz, 165 Hz or 180 Hz specification does not guarantee that every device will output that mode. HDMI and DisplayPort capabilities depend on both devices, their versions, the cable and the chosen resolution. Check the manual for the exact supported modes. Console and laptop capabilities may differ.

Response-time claims are not standardized across brands. Independent reviews are more informative than a single advertised millisecond figure.

3. Panel quality and comfort

IPS panels often offer useful viewing angles, but contrast, brightness, color accuracy and motion handling vary widely by model. Look for measured performance when editing photos or playing fast games. Adaptive sync can reduce tearing when supported by the monitor and the connected device.

  • Check actual HDMI and DisplayPort inputs and supported resolutions.
  • Confirm VESA support and height adjustment before buying an arm.
  • Consider brightness, glare, warranty and pixel-defect policies.

4. Compare the full cost

A monitor may require an additional compatible cable or desk mount. Include these costs along with shipping and any regional warranty differences. Compare the exact model number and avoid assuming that all similarly named versions share the same ports or stand.
